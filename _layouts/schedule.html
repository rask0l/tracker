checklist

    ↓ Fri 9/25 · work day · split: ~50m on the clock, ~2h at home

    Two halves. Work hours take the no-traffic work (Anki, reading, writeups) on sanctioned training time. The labs wait until you're home and off the office network. Work-hours times are approximate, shift them to your real lunch and gaps.

    • 12:30Lunch: Anki, 15 min. Cap the deck at 20 cards before you open it. No VPN, no lab, just cards.on clock
    • 15:00First gap: read the HTB report template end to end. Pure reading, nothing to connect to. Clears an exam_prep gate that's been sitting undone.on clock
    • 18:30Home. Eat, shower, decompress. Don't open the laptop yet.
    • 19:30Sprint 1 + 2 (25/5). VPN up, open the SQLi labs, attack first, read only to unblock.labs
    • 20:30Break, 10 min. Stand up, off screens.
    • 20:40Sprint 3 + 4. Keep attacking. This is the target for the night.labs
    • 21:40Wind-down, 5 min. Note where you stopped, then log the day in cwes.yml. Leave the writeup for tomorrow on purpose.
    • 21:45Done. Screens down. No study guilt after this.
    • The loop this sets up. Evening does the labs, the next work day does the retrieval on that lab work: closed-book writeup, then gaps into Anki cards. That keeps do-first intact (you still attack before you read), moves the low-dopamine writing onto sanctioned work time instead of making it compete with your evening, and puts encoding and retrieval in separate sessions, which is the spacing effect doing the work for free.
    • Keep the labs off the office network. Training being approved isn't the same as approval to run offensive tooling over work WiFi. A dropped or split-tunnelled HTB VPN can put scan traffic on the corporate LAN, which is a bad look for a pentester even when it's an accident. Worth asking whoever signed off on the training whether lab traffic is in scope, since sanctioned time probably means they'll just say yes or point you at a guest network.
    • Floor and ceiling. Two evening sprints is a real day and it counts. Four is the target. Stopping at two is not a failed evening, and treating it as one is exactly the move that turned a single missed day into eight weeks last time.
    • Protect the sleep. A short night costs an ADHD brain disproportionately more than it costs everyone else. If it's 22:00 and you're deciding whether to push on, the answer is no.

    Win condition: one working SQL injection you found with your own hands. Everything else is volume.
